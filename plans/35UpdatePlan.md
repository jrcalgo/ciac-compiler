# CIaC v0.35-file — The Wrapper Is Not The Work: Deleting The Build Tool From The Inner Loop, On Four Targets That Never Needed It There (implementation plan)

> Implementation plan. Document number ≠ release number (standing
> precedent; expected to ship as **0.33.0**). Assumes
> `26UpdatePlan.md`–`34UpdatePlan.md` shipped, the last of them as
> 0.32.0.
>
> This arc has one subject: the **per-scenario invocation path** inside
> `ciac sim`'s ten `sim_drive_*` functions
> (`crates/ciac/src/commands.rs`). Four of the five targets build an
> artifact once and then, for every scenario, invoke the *build tool*
> again to run it — `cargo run`, `go run`, `uv run`, `./mvnw
> exec:java` — when the artifact is already built and its path is
> already knowable. The build tool's job ended at the build. Every one
> of those re-invocations re-does the build tool's own startup work:
> cargo re-fingerprints a 6.2 GB target tree, `go run` re-links, `uv
> run` re-validates a venv that `uv sync` just synchronised, and
> `exec:java` pays a full Maven bootstrap. The fifth target,
> TypeScript, already does the right thing (`commands.rs:2008-2011`,
> `node dist/sim_runner.js`) — so this arc is a **consistency
> argument**, not a novel design.
>
> **The premise correction that makes this arc necessary reading before
> its predecessor.** `34UpdatePlan.md` opens on "rust is 63% of the
> run," and every optimization idea downstream of it reasons from that
> split. That measurement was taken *before* its own lever 1 landed and
> **is now wrong**. Its M2 measured rust's isolated stream at
> **380.89s** against a whole-run time of ~940s: rust is **~40%**, and
> the other four targets are collectively **~60%**. Lever 1 succeeded
> well enough to invert the premise. This document states that
> correction first, in its own section, because a reader who arrives
> here via `34UpdatePlan.md`'s handoff will otherwise carry a stale
> model of where the time goes.
>
> **What this arc deliberately does not do.** `34UpdatePlan.md`'s
> retrospective (`:1973-1987`) hands off two concurrency mechanisms —
> bounded streams below the core count, and rust-first two-phase
> scheduling — as "a materially different mechanism deserving its own
> pre-registered threshold rather than a rescue of this one." **This
> arc takes neither**, and the reason is not timidity: 35 changes the
> serial denominator that any future concurrency arc would be measured
> against, so running it first makes that arc's eventual number more
> honest, not less. Mixing the two mechanism classes in one document
> would also make a disappointing cumulative figure un-attributable,
> which is precisely the failure `34UpdatePlan.md`'s own per-lever
> structure was built to avoid.
>
> **Contract A — the harness's verdict is preserved exactly.** The same
> 50 program×target combinations, driving the same 15 scenarios per
> target, producing the same `[PASS]`/`[FAIL]` per scenario, with the
> same exit-code semantics. A run that would fail today must fail after
> this arc, for the same reason, naming the same combination.
> Mechanically checkable by sorted diff against M1's recorded report,
> exactly as `34UpdatePlan.md` M3 established — and that check earned
> its place there by catching a real bug (`slot_id()` stripping
> `.ciac`) that no timing measurement would ever have surfaced.
>
> **Contract B — no generated byte changes, with one pre-registered,
> enumerated, comment-only exception.** This is the **first arc in the
> series to carve an exception into Contract B**, and the document
> treats that as a serious act rather than a convenience. The exception
> is bounded to java snapshots, bounded to comments, counted in advance
> (27), and spent by exactly one milestone (M6). Its full terms have
> their own top-level section below. Every other target's snapshots —
> python, rust, go, typescript — remain under the absolute rule with no
> graduated reading.
>
> **Contract C — no metric may regress to buy another.** Every lever in
> this arc touches `crates/ciac/src/commands.rs`, so the full
> `ciac-bench --compare` sweep is owed at the checkpoint, reviewed in
> its entirety rather than only on targeted rows. A milestone that
> improves its own metric while pushing another outside its noise band
> is a trade, and the trade must be stated and accepted at that
> milestone rather than discovered at close-out.
>
> **Contract D — the harness must still be able to fail, and this is
> proven, not assumed.** Inherited from `34UpdatePlan.md` unchanged,
> both injection paths at every milestone boundary. The document notes
> honestly that its predecessor's M4 row is an **empty, disclosed gap**
> (`34UpdatePlan.md:1877`) — the milestone was reverted before its
> fault-injection pass ran — and pre-registers that this arc does not
> repeat that pattern: a lever that is going to be reverted still gets
> its Contract D pass *before* the revert, because the revert decision
> and the correctness evidence are independent products.
>
> **Confidence.** High on M2 — a pure refactor with a byte-identity
> exit criterion is the most checkable milestone shape this series has.
> High on M3's mechanism and moderate on its blast radius: cargo's JSON
> artifact output is a stable, documented interface, but routing the
> build through it touches `run_in_shared_cargo`, whose `run_streamed`
> path (`:192`) has a `JSON_MODE` branch that must not be contaminated.
> High on M4 — `go build -o <path>` followed by exec is the least
> subtle change in the arc. Moderate-to-low on M5's *magnitude*
> specifically: uv is a Rust binary and fast, so the per-call saving
> may be 100–300 ms, and this document says so rather than implying
> four equal levers. **Lowest on M6, which is why it is gated**: its
> mechanism is proven (its own `_multi` sibling already runs it) but
> its arithmetic trades N Maven calls for N JVM starts at the cost of
> one extra Maven call per program, and for six of seven single-service
> java programs that is a wash. Every number below was measured on this
> machine (4 vCPU, 16 GB) — **ratios are expected to travel to other
> hardware; absolute timings are not**, and no threshold in this
> document is stated as an absolute for that reason.

## The premise correction: rust is no longer the majority

`34UpdatePlan.md`'s headline table reads:

| Target | Time | Share |
|---|---|---|
| **rust** | **48.3s** | **63%** |
| typescript | 9.8s | 13% |
| python | 7.9s | 10% |
| java | 7.5s | 10% |
| go | 3.2s | 4% |

That table was produced by timing **one program** (`quickstart.ciac`,
the smallest in the corpus) against each target, *before* lever 1
existed, and multiplying. It was correct when written and it is wrong
now, in two independent ways.

**First, lever 1 removed most of what made rust dominant.** The whole
point of `34UpdatePlan.md` M2 was that rust recompiled its entire
dependency tree ten times per run because each generated project had a
private `target/` that the harness then deleted. Sharing one persistent
target directory took rust's steady-state per-program cost from 48.3s
to ~15s — a measured **3.07×**. A 63% share does not survive a 3×
reduction in the thing that produced it.

**Second, the 76.7s-per-program extrapolation was itself low.** M1's
own measured full-run baseline came in at **1305.4s**, roughly 1.7×
the pre-code estimate of ~764s — a discrepancy `34UpdatePlan.md`'s M1
recorded and deliberately declined to assign an unmeasured cause to
(its Pillar 1 clause forbids that). The per-program figures were taken
on the corpus's *smallest* program; the other nine are larger.

**What the post-lever-1 split actually is.** M2 measured rust's
isolated stream — the full ten-program rust column, via `--targets
rust` — at **380.89s**. M6's whole-run re-measurement was **929.4s**,
M8's close-out run **949.3s**. Subtracting:

| | Time | Share of ~940s |
|---|---|---|
| rust (measured, `--targets rust`) | **380.89s** | **~40%** |
| the other four, collectively (derived) | **~560s** | **~60%** |

**This subtraction is a derivation, not a measurement, and the document
labels it as such** — M1 of this arc re-measures the per-target
decomposition directly rather than inheriting arithmetic, because the
inherited decomposition predates lever 1 and the derived figure has
never been checked against five individual `--targets` runs.

The consequence for this arc's design is direct: **an optimization that
only helps rust can now reach at most ~40% of the run.** The four
levers below are deliberately spread across all five targets for that
reason, and the one target left alone (TypeScript) is left alone
because it is already correct, not because it is small.

## The gap this version closes

`ciac sim` compiles a generated project and then runs its simulator
against one or more scenario files. The corpus carries **15 scenarios
across 10 programs**, so five programs drive the runner more than once:

| Program | Scenarios | Multi-service |
|---|---|---|
| `sim-peripherals` | **4** | no |
| `sim-vertical-slice` | **2** | no |
| `domain-orders` | **2** | no |
| `sim-broker-slice` | 1 | no |
| `query-verbs` | 1 | no |
| `order-system` | 1 | no |
| `quickstart` | 1 | no |
| `sim-three-service` | 1 | **yes** |
| `multi-service-media` | 1 | **yes** |
| `inventory-system` | 1 | **yes** |

(`scripts/sim-corpus-x5.sh:73-84`.) Ten programs × five targets = 50
combinations; 15 scenarios per target = **75 scenario-runs per full
harness pass**.

Every one of those 75 scenario-runs, on four of the five targets,
starts a build tool that has nothing left to build.

### What each target does per scenario, today

| Target | Build step (once per program) | **Per-scenario invocation** | What the wrapper re-does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **rust** | `cargo build -q --bin sim_runner` (`:1787`) | **`cargo run -q --bin sim_runner --`** (`:1792-1800`) | re-fingerprints the whole dependency graph against a **6.2 GB** shared target dir |
| **go** | `go build -o /dev/null ./cmd/sim_runner` (`:2090-2094`) | **`go run ./cmd/sim_runner`** (`:2095-2100`, `:2154-2155`) | recompiles *and re-links* into a temp dir; the earlier build's output went to `/dev/null` |
| **python** | `uv sync -q` (`:1547`) | **`uv run python <driver>`** (`:1555-1566`) | re-reads `pyproject.toml`/`uv.lock`, re-validates a venv synced moments earlier |
| **java** | `./mvnw -q -B test-compile` (`:2261`) | **`./mvnw -q -B exec:java`** (`:2267-2272`) | a full Maven bootstrap: JVM start, plugin resolution, project model build |
| **typescript** | `npm ci` + `npm run build` (`:1951-1952`) | **`node dist/sim_runner.js`** (`:2008-2011`) | **nothing — this is the correct shape** |

TypeScript is not an outlier to be explained away; it is the existing
in-repo answer. `run_node_sim_runner` (`:2000`) takes the built
artifact's path and executes it. That is what the other four should do,
and three of them have an in-repo precedent for doing it (below).

### Go is the clearest waste: it compiles twice and discards the first

`sim_drive_go_single` (`:2078`) runs:

```rust
run_in(
    project_dir,
    "go",
    &["build", "-o", "/dev/null", "./cmd/sim_runner"],
)?;
run_go_sim_runner(
    project_dir,
    &["run", "./cmd/sim_runner"],
    scenarios,
    wall_timeout,
)
```

The first invocation compiles and links the runner, then throws the
linked output away — it functions purely as an error check. `go run`
then compiles and links it **again**, into a temporary directory, once
per scenario. `GOCACHE`/`GOMODCACHE` cover package compilation across
both, but **not the final link**, which is repeated every time.
`sim_drive_go_multi` (`:2118`) has the identical pair at `:2135-2136`
with `.` as the package selector instead of `./cmd/sim_runner`.

Building to a real path and executing it directly removes one whole
link per program (the discarded one) *and* the per-scenario re-link.

### The repository already contains one of these fixes

This is the single most important structural fact in this document, and
it is why three of the four levers are propagation rather than
invention.

**`sim_drive_java_multi` (`:2327`) already does exactly what is being
proposed for `sim_drive_java_single`.** It:

1. runs `./mvnw -q -B test-compile` per service project (`:2349`);
2. runs `./mvnw -q -B dependency:build-classpath
   -Dmdep.outputFile=…` once per project and reads the result
   (`:2350-2368`);
3. assembles a full classpath from `target/classes`,
   `target/test-classes`, and the resolved dependencies (`:2371-2378`);
4. compiles the system runner with **`javac`** directly (`:2388-2398`);
5. and then, per scenario, invokes **bare `java -cp <classpath>
   SystemSimRunner <scenario>`** (`:2404-2407`) — with **no Maven
   anywhere in the inner loop**.

Meanwhile `sim_drive_java_single`, 78 lines earlier in the same file
and serving the same target, still runs `./mvnw -q -B exec:java` once
per scenario.

Same file. Same target. Same problem. Two different answers, one of
which is already the right one. `34UpdatePlan.md`'s Pillar 8 ("reuse
before invention") applies with unusual directness here: the design
work for lever D was done by `28UpdatePlan.md` M8 and simply never
propagated sideways.

The same pattern holds for python: `sim_drive_python_multi` already
globs `dir/.venv/lib/*/site-packages` (`:1662-1672`) to assemble a
`PYTHONPATH`, so **the code already assumes `.venv` sits at the project
root and already reaches inside it**. Deriving `.venv/bin/python` is
strictly less inference than what that function performs today.

### The four levers, stated

| Lever | Target | Change | In-repo precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | rust | build once, exec the built binary per scenario | TypeScript's `run_node_sim_runner` (`:2000`) |
| **B** | go | `go build -o <real path>`, exec it per scenario | TypeScript, same |
| **C** | python | `uv sync`, then exec `.venv/bin/python` per scenario | `python_multi`'s own venv glob (`:1662-1672`) |
| **D** | java | classpath once, `java -cp` per scenario | **`java_multi` itself** (`:2346-2407`) |

All four converge on one shape — *prepare once, resolve an executable,
exec per scenario* — which is why M2 lands the shared helper first and
every lever afterward is a small diff against a single call site.

### The arithmetic this arc *can* state: invocation counts

This document declines to project a speedup (below). It does not
decline to count, and the count is exact rather than estimated —
derived from the corpus definition (`sim-corpus-x5.sh:73-84`) and the
driver source, both of which are fixed and readable.

One full harness pass drives **50 combinations** and **75
scenario-runs**. Here is every build-tool invocation it makes, before
and after, per target:

| Target | Today: build calls | Today: **wrapper calls in the scenario loop** | After: build calls | After: wrapper calls | Δ wrapper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **rust** | 10 × `cargo build` | **15 × `cargo run`** | 10 × `cargo build` | 0 | **−15** |
| **go** | 10 × `go build -o /dev/null` | **15 × `go run`** | 10 × `go build -o <path>` | 0 | **−15** |
| **python** | 10 × `uv sync` | **15 × `uv run`** | 10 × `uv sync` | 0 | **−15** |
| **java** (`_single`) | 7 × `test-compile` | **12 × `mvnw exec:java`** | 7 × `test-compile` + **7 × `build-classpath`** | 0 | **−12, +7 = −5 net** |
| **java** (`_multi`) | already correct | 0 | unchanged | 0 | 0 |
| **typescript** | 10 × `npm ci` + 10 × `npm run build` | **0 — already correct** | unchanged | 0 | 0 |
| | | | | **Total** | **−50** |

**Fifty-seven build-tool invocations leave the inner loop; seven enter
it.** Net fifty fewer per harness pass.

Three properties of that table are worth stating explicitly, because
they are what make the levers unequal:

**Rust's fifteen are the most expensive per call.** Each `cargo run`
re-fingerprints the dependency graph against a **6.2 GB** shared target
directory (measured, this session) before exec'ing. That is the largest
tree of the four, and the fingerprint scan stats every source file of
every dependency.

**Go's fifteen are the only ones that redo compilation.** `cargo run`,
`uv run`, and `exec:java` all re-validate and then run an artifact that
already exists. `go run` compiles and links into a temporary directory
every time. Go additionally has the only wasted *build*: the `-o
/dev/null` invocation produces a linked binary and discards it, so
lever B removes work in two places rather than one, and its per-program
saving is not captured by the per-scenario marginal cost at all. M4
measures that half separately for exactly this reason.

**Java is the only row with a positive term.** Seven
`dependency:build-classpath` calls are *added* — one per single-service
program — to remove twelve `exec:java` calls. Whether that trade is
favourable depends entirely on the ratio between a Maven bootstrap's
cost and a bare JVM start's cost, which is the quantity M1 measures and
the gate turns on. **For a program with one scenario, the trade is one
Maven call for one Maven call plus one JVM start — strictly worse.**
Six of java's seven single-service programs have exactly one scenario.
The entire lever rests on `sim-peripherals` (4 scenarios),
`sim-vertical-slice` (2), and `domain-orders` (2) carrying enough
weight to overcome six unfavourable trades.

That last observation is the strongest argument for gating lever D at
M1 rather than implementing it and measuring afterward, and it is why
this document gates it.

### What is deliberately not claimed

**This document states no projected speedup for any lever.** That is a
deviation from `33UpdatePlan.md`'s and `34UpdatePlan.md`'s house style,
both of which opened with a measured "what this is worth" table, and it
is taken deliberately rather than by omission.

The reason is Pillar 1. Every prior arc in this series derived its
pre-registered thresholds from measurements taken *before* the document
was written. Here, the quantity that determines all four levers' value
— **the marginal cost of one additional scenario, per target** — had
not been isolated when this document was authored. Estimating it from
first principles ("Maven startup is ~3s, so…") is exactly the
unmeasured-premise reasoning that `32UpdatePlan.md` M8 got wrong and
that Pillar 1's measurement clause exists to forbid.

So M1 measures it, and **M1's measurement sets the thresholds for M3,
M4, M5, and M6**, using a derivation rule fixed in advance (below) so
that the numbers cannot be fitted to whatever the levers happen to
deliver. This is a stricter reading of Pillar 1 than its predecessors
applied to themselves, and the document flags it as such rather than
presenting it as business as usual.

## What was verified in source before this plan was written

A plan built on a profile nobody re-read is the failure mode this
series exists to prevent. Every claim below was checked against the
actual code and is cited so the implementer need not re-derive it.

**The four wrapper invocations, and their `_multi` twins.** Each lever
must be applied to *both* driver variants or it leaves part of the
corpus uncovered — the same trap `34UpdatePlan.md` M2 named when it
noted that fixing only `sim_drive_rust_single` would have left the
three multi-service programs (30% of the rust stream) uncached.

| Target | `_single` | build | per-scenario wrapper | `_multi` | build | per-scenario wrapper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rust | `:1775` | `:1787` | `:1792-1800` | `:1842` | `:1859` | `:1864-1870` |
| go | `:2078` | `:2090-2094` | `:2095-2100` → `:2154-2155` | `:2118` | `:2135` | `:2136` → `:2154-2155` |
| python | `:1536` | `:1547` | `:1555-1566` | `:1620` | `:1656` | `:1687-1700` |
| java | `:2249` | `:2261` | `:2267-2272` | `:2327` | `:2349-2398` | **`:2404-2407` (already correct)** |

**The shared cargo target directory is 6.2 GB on this machine.**
Measured directly: `du -sh /tmp/ciac-verify-cargo-target` → `6.2G`.
`shared_cargo_target_dir()` (`:3741`) returns
`std::env::temp_dir().join("ciac-verify-cargo-target")`, and
`apply_shared_cargo_target_dir` (`:3754`) applies it guarded by
`std::env::var_os("CARGO_TARGET_DIR").is_none()`. Lever A's per-scenario
`cargo run` re-stats that entire tree's fingerprints before exec'ing.

**`run_streamed` has a `JSON_MODE` branch that lever A must not
contaminate.** `run_streamed` (`:192`) checks
`JSON_MODE.load(Ordering::Relaxed)` and, when set, captures the child's
output and writes **both stdout and stderr to stderr**. `ciac sim
--json` sets that flag via `with_json_envelope` (`:212`). Lever A
changes the build invocation to `--message-format=json-render-diagnostics`
in order to read the artifact path — so the artifact JSON now appears
on the build's stdout, and under `JSON_MODE` that stdout is currently
redirected to stderr. **The lever must capture the build's stdout for
parsing rather than letting `run_streamed` redirect it**, and the
`--json` envelope must be verified clean afterward. This is FM3 in the
register below.

**`run_go_sim_runner` takes a `go run` package selector, not a binary.**
`:2145-2155`: it accepts `args: &[&str]` and does
`Command::new("go").args(args).arg(&scenario_abs)`. Its doc comment
(`:2139-2144`) states the contract explicitly — "`args` carries the
caller's own `go run` package selector (`./cmd/sim_runner` for
single-service, `.` for `system-runner`)". Lever B changes this
signature, which is the one API break in the arc and is confined to two
call sites (`:2095-2100`, `:2136`).

**`cfg!(windows)` is already an established idiom in this file.**
`sim_drive_java_multi:2346` uses `let sep = if cfg!(windows) { ";" }
else { ":" };`. Lever B's `.exe` suffix and lever C's
`Scripts/python.exe` follow that precedent rather than introducing a
platform-branching style.

**The java main class is template-derived, and the POM states it.**
`crates/ciac-backend-java/templates/pom.xml.j2:237` reads
`<mainClass>com.ciac.{{ java_package }}.sim.SimRunner</mainClass>`.
Lever D must resolve that FQCN without parsing XML and without coupling
the driver to codegen's package derivation — resolved in the open
questions below.

**Java dependency sets differ between programs, so classpaths cannot be
shared blindly.** `pom.xml.j2:37-45` gates
`exec-maven-plugin.version` on `{%- if … %}` and pins logging
dependencies under `{%- if c.has_logging %}`. Two generated projects do
not necessarily resolve the same classpath. This kills the obvious
"compute the classpath once for the whole corpus" optimization and is
recorded under Explicit cuts rather than silently omitted.

**The parse-and-bail block is duplicated ten times.** The ~25-line
"capture output → take the last stdout line → bail if empty → parse
JSON → bail with context" sequence appears at `:1574-1601`
(python single), `:1712-1739` (python multi), `:1802-1828` (rust
single), `:1872-1899` (rust multi), `:2013-2040` (node, serving both TS
variants), `:2157-2184` (go, serving both go variants), `:2274-2300`
(java single), and `:2409-2435` (java multi). Every copy implements the
same one-line-JSON-on-stdout protocol. M2 collapses them.

**`wall_timeout` is `None` on the CLI path.** Verified in
`34UpdatePlan.md`'s own source audit and unchanged: the CLI passes
`wall_timeout: None` (`:2459`, and the `--json` variant at `:2498`);
the 60-second `MCP_SIM_WALL_TIMEOUT` (`:2524`) is used only by the MCP
path (`:2567`). No lever in this arc can convert a slower invocation
into a failure — only into a longer one. Relevant because it removes an
entire class of risk from all four levers at once.

**The harness deletes each project between combinations.**
`scripts/sim-corpus-x5.sh:111` and `:116` bracket the `ciac sim`
invocation with `rm -rf "$out"`. Every build under the harness is
therefore cold with respect to the generated project (though warm with
respect to the shared cargo target dir and the other toolchains'
`$HOME` caches). This is what makes FM1's stale-binary risk
harness-proof, and it is also why the uncovered case is a *developer*
re-running `ciac sim` against a persistent `--out`.

**`scripts/sim-corpus-x5.sh` is structurally serial and stays that
way.** Its header comment (`:20-34`) records `34UpdatePlan.md` M4's
reverted parallelism and states "This script therefore remains
structurally serial." This arc touches the script only at M8, to
refresh that comment.

**Version and snapshot inventory.** `Cargo.toml:21` is `version =
"0.32.0"`. `tests/tests/snapshots/` holds **145** `golden__gen__*.snap`
files, of which **29** are `golden__gen__java__*.snap`, of which **27**
contain the literal `exec:java`.

### The quantity this arc turns on, and why it is not yet known

Every lever deletes *per-scenario wrapper overhead*. That quantity has
never been isolated in this repository. It is not visible in any
existing measurement, because every timing this series has taken is a
whole-run or whole-stream figure in which build cost and scenario cost
are summed.

**M1 isolates it by differencing two programs that differ only in
scenario count.** `sim-peripherals` carries 4 scenarios; `quickstart`
carries 1. Both are single-service. Timing each against one target
gives:

```
marginal_cost_per_scenario ≈ (T(sim-peripherals) − T(quickstart)) / 3
```

with the caveat — stated at M1, not discovered later — that the two
programs also differ in *build* cost, so the difference is an
**upper bound** on per-scenario cost, not a point estimate. M1
tightens it by additionally timing `domain-orders` (2 scenarios) and
`sim-vertical-slice` (2 scenarios), giving four points across three
distinct scenario counts per target and letting the slope be fitted
rather than assumed from two readings.

That slope, per target, is what M3–M6's thresholds are derived from.

## Measurement methodology

A ~940s harness is expensive to measure, and the temptation to
under-sample is the single most likely way this arc produces a number
it should not trust. `33UpdatePlan.md` caught contaminated readings
three separate times — most consequentially at its own M1, where four
independent readings of one quantity spanned 135s to 185s and the
committed baseline turned out to be 37% high on the binary that
mattered most. This arc inherits that lesson as binding procedure
rather than advice.

**Isolation.** Nothing else runs on the machine during a timing run.
Not a build, not a test suite, not another shell doing something
harmless. `34UpdatePlan.md`'s execution observed a ~50% inflation on
two test binaries measured while a background `sim-corpus` run was
still in flight; those readings were discarded and retaken rather than
reported.

**Disk.** Free space is checked and brought below 80% utilisation
before any timing run. This is not a formality here: the machine is at
**74% with 9.7 GB free**, and the shared cargo target directory alone
is **6.2 GB**. Disk pressure changes cargo's and npm's behaviour in
ways that look like performance noise but are not.

**Repetitions.** Two for full runs, three for anything cheaper. The
2-rep choice is a **deliberate, disclosed deviation** from this series'
3-rep norm, inherited from `34UpdatePlan.md` and justified by each full
run costing ~940s. Where a decision turns on a margin narrower than the
observed spread between the two reps, M7 takes a third rather than
deciding on two. Every per-target and per-program measurement — which
is to say every input to M1's threshold derivation — takes three.

**Decomposition.** Per-target figures come from five separate
`--targets <one>` runs rather than from instrumenting the harness. They
sum to the same total work, they need no code change to obtain, and
they give exactly the stream-level numbers the thresholds are computed
against. This is the same reasoning that made `--targets` worth having.

**The marginal-cost fit, in detail.** For each target, time four
single-service programs individually, three reps each:

| Program | Scenarios | Role in the fit |
|---|---|---|
| `quickstart` | 1 | the intercept-anchoring point |
| `domain-orders` | 2 | |
| `sim-vertical-slice` | 2 | a second 2-scenario point, so the fit is not a line through two counts |
| `sim-peripherals` | 4 | the lever arm |

Fit `T = b + m·n` across those points, where `n` is scenario count.
`m` is the per-scenario marginal cost; `b` absorbs build cost and
process setup. **The two 2-scenario programs are included specifically
so that build-cost variation between programs shows up as residual
scatter rather than being silently absorbed into the slope** — if
`domain-orders` and `sim-vertical-slice` disagree substantially at the
same `n`, the fit's `m` is unreliable and M1 says so rather than
reporting a slope it does not believe.

**Record the residuals, not just the slope.** M1's exit criterion
includes the fit's residuals per target, so a reader can judge whether
the derived thresholds rest on a clean linear relationship or on noise
wearing a regression's clothing. If a target's residuals are large
relative to `m`, that target's threshold is flagged as
low-confidence in the running log and its lever's Pillar 5 decision
requires a third rep at its own boundary.

**Separately: one Maven bootstrap's cost.** M1 times a single
`./mvnw -q -B dependency:build-classpath` on a generated java project,
three reps, because lever D's threshold charges seven of them and the
gate turns on whether a Maven bootstrap materially exceeds a bare JVM
start. M1 also times a bare `java -cp … SimRunner <scenario>` against
an already-compiled project, three reps, so both sides of that trade
are measured rather than one side being inferred.

**What is compared to what.** Each lever's threshold is a per-target
stream figure measured against M1's re-measured per-target
decomposition — deliberately *not* against the whole run, because a
15s saving on the go stream is invisible in a ~940s total and would be
credited or blamed by noise from four other targets. The cumulative
whole-run figure is computed once, at M7, over whatever levers
survived. Every ratio in the running log states both of its operands.

## The five drivers, walked through

The implementer will be editing all ten driver functions, so their
current anatomy is recorded here rather than left to be re-derived.

**Python, single-service (`:1536-1603`).** Runs `uv sync -q` in the
project (`:1547`), builds a `PYTHONPATH` joining the sim directory and
the project directory (`:1549-1550`), then loops scenarios: each
iteration constructs `uv run python <sim_dir>/auto_driver.py <plan>
<scenario> --source-hash … --plan-hash …` with optional `--record`/
`--replay`, sets `PYTHONPATH`, runs it captured, writes any stderr
through, takes the last stdout line, and parses it as
`SimScenarioOutcome`.

**Python, multi-service (`:1620-1745`).** Resolves each declared service
to its generated project directory by kebab-case basename
(`:1642-1655`), runs `uv sync -q` per service (`:1656`), then assembles
a `PYTHONPATH` union by globbing every project's
`.venv/lib/*/site-packages` (`:1660-1675`). Picks the first service's
project as the driver project (`:1682`, with a doc comment explaining
the choice is arbitrary-but-deterministic), then loops scenarios
invoking `uv run --project <driver_project> python
<sim_dir>/multi_driver.py …` with a `--service name=dir` argument per
service.

**Rust, single-service (`:1775-1831`).** Guards on
`src/bin/sim_runner.rs` existing (`:1780-1786`), builds via
`run_in_shared_cargo(project_dir, &["build", "-q", "--bin",
"sim_runner"])` (`:1787`), then loops scenarios running `cargo run -q
--bin sim_runner -- <scenario>` with `apply_shared_cargo_target_dir`
(`:1792-1800`).

**Rust, multi-service (`:1842-1905`).** Guards on
`system-runner/src/main.rs` (`:1849-1858`), builds with
`run_in_shared_cargo(&runner_dir, &["build", "-q"])` (`:1859`) — note
**no `--bin` selector**, so the binary name is whatever
`system-runner`'s `Cargo.toml` declares — then loops `cargo run -q --`
(`:1864-1870`). This is the case that makes convention-based path
derivation awkward and JSON artifact parsing the better answer.

**TypeScript (`:1939-1998`, `:2000-2046`).** `_single` guards on
`src/sim_runner.ts`, runs `npm ci` then `npm run build` (`:1951-1952`),
and delegates to `run_node_sim_runner`, which loops scenarios invoking
`node dist/sim_runner.js <scenario>` (`:2008-2011`). `_multi` builds
`sim-shared`, then each service, then `system-runner`, in that order
(per its doc comment at `:1956-1966`, which records the live finding
that npm does not transitively build `file:` dependencies), then
delegates to the same helper. **Nothing here changes in this arc.**

**Go (`:2078-2101`, `:2118-2137`, `:2145-2192`).** Both variants guard
on their entry point, run `go build -o /dev/null <pkg>`, then delegate
to `run_go_sim_runner` with a `go run <pkg>` argument vector, which
loops scenarios appending the scenario path.

**Java, single-service (`:2249-2325`).** Guards via
`java_sim_runner_exists`, runs `./mvnw -q -B test-compile` (`:2261`),
then loops scenarios invoking `./mvnw -q -B exec:java
-Dexec.args=<scenario>` (`:2267-2272`).

**Java, multi-service (`:2327-2438`).** The correct one. Detailed above.

**What the rewrite must preserve exactly, in all ten:** the
one-line-JSON-on-stdout protocol; stderr pass-through when the child
writes any (`:1580-1583` and its eight twins); the "exited with {} and
printed no result on stdout" bail text; the "cannot parse …'s result
for scenario {}: {last_line:?}" bail text; the per-driver guard
messages and their `docs/simulation.md` references; and
`wall_timeout` threading. Contract A is checkable precisely because all
of that is mechanical.

## What this arc does not do

It does not add concurrency, retry `34UpdatePlan.md`'s reverted lever
2, or introduce any bash parallelism. The harness stays structurally

It does not change which combinations run. The corpus

and so is 75.

It does not change what any target generates, with the single

It does not wire the harness into CI. It stays run-on-demand

## Pillar 1 — Every threshold is pre-registered, and here that means derived from M1 by a rule fixed in advance

Inherited from `32UpdatePlan.md`–`34UpdatePlan.md`, whose statement of
the failure mode is not improved by rewording: "a threshold chosen
after seeing the data is a threshold fitted to the data, and it will be
chosen — consciously or not — to make the current state pass."

`33UpdatePlan.md` added the clause this arc leans on hardest: **a root
cause offered for a missed threshold is itself a claim, and it gets a
measurement or it gets labelled a hypothesis.** That clause exists
because `32UpdatePlan.md` M8 explained a shortfall by reasoning from an
unmeasured premise about hardware, and was wrong.

**This arc applies Pillar 1 to itself more strictly than its
predecessors did**, and the difference is worth stating plainly. Prior
arcs measured their levers' mechanisms before writing their documents,
then pre-registered thresholds as fractions of the measured ceiling
(M2's `≥2×` against a measured 3.2× ceiling, for instance). This
document cannot do that, because the governing quantity — per-scenario
wrapper overhead — was never isolated.

The honest response is not to guess a threshold and call it
pre-registered. It is to **pre-register the derivation rule** and let
M1 supply its inputs:

> For each target `t`, let `m_t` be M1's fitted marginal cost per
> scenario, and `S_t` the target's total stream time from M1's
> re-measured decomposition. The lever's *ideal* saving is `m_t × 15`
> for rust, go, and python (all 15 scenarios pass through the wrapper)
> and `m_t × 12` for java (its three multi-service programs already
> use the fixed path). The pre-registered threshold is **60% of that
> ideal**, expressed as a ratio on the target's stream:
>
> ```
> threshold_t  =  S_t / (S_t − 0.6 × m_t × N_t)
> ```
>
> with `N_t` = 15 (rust, go, python) or 12 (java). The 60% factor
> accounts for the fact that a directly-exec'd binary still pays
> process startup, dynamic linking, and the runner's own initialisation
> — the wrapper's overhead is what is removed, not the whole marginal
> cost.
>
> Java additionally charges **+7 `dependency:build-classpath`
> invocations** against its saving before the ratio is computed, since
> lever D adds one Maven call per single-service program. M1 measures
> the cost of one `build-classpath` call directly for this purpose.

The half-point for revert is `(T + 1) / 2`, inherited verbatim from
`33UpdatePlan.md`'s Pillar 5 (which added it mid-arc after an earlier
draft stated an incoherent half-point of "0.9×" for a speedup ratio).

**The rule above is fixed now, before M1 runs.** It cannot be adjusted
after seeing `m_t`. If it produces a threshold that a lever obviously
cannot clear, that is information about the lever, not a reason to
change the rule — and Pillar 5's table then applies as written.

## Pillar 2 — The harness's verdict is the contract, and it is machine-checked by forcing a failure

Contract A says the harness's output is preserved. The mechanism that
proves it is not inspection — it is deliberately breaking things.

The reason this pillar exists separately from the contract is inherited
unchanged from `34UpdatePlan.md`: for every other kind of change in
this repository, a break announces itself. A moved golden snapshot
fails loudly. A nondeterministic generation fails `determinism.rs`
loudly. But a harness whose failure detection is broken **passes
everything, prints a reassuring message, and exits 0**.

So at every milestone boundary, without exception:

1. Run `scripts/sim-corpus-x5.sh` unmodified. It must exit 0 and report
   all 50 combinations green.
2. **Injection A — the scenario path.** Perturb one expected value in
   `sim/quickstart.ciac-sim.json` so the simulator runs to completion
   and disagrees. Pre-registered expectation, from
   `34UpdatePlan.md`'s M3 and M6 rows: exit **1**, and the report reads
   `sim-corpus-x5: 5 failing combination(s), 5 failing scenario(s), 0
   missing result(s), out of 50 combinations.` — five combinations
   (one per target), five scenario mismatches, correctly separated
   counts.
3. **Injection B — the process path.** Point the harness's `CIAC`
   variable (`sim-corpus-x5.sh:57`) at a nonexistent binary.
   Pre-registered expectation: exit **1** in ~1s, report reads
   `sim-corpus-x5: 50 failing combination(s), 0 failing scenario(s), 0
   missing result(s), out of 50 combinations.`, all 50 rows carrying
   `(build/refusal)`.
4. Revert both injections and re-confirm green.

The two expectations are deliberately different in shape (5/5 versus
50/0), so a tally that has silently merged its units cannot satisfy
both.

**This arc does not repeat its predecessor's one disclosed gap.**
`34UpdatePlan.md:1877` records that M4's Contract D row is empty — the
milestone was reverted per Pillar 5 before its fault-injection pass ran,
so its accounting addition was never adversarially tested. That was
recorded honestly and had no live consequence because the code was
reverted. **This document pre-registers the opposite ordering: Contract
D runs at a milestone's boundary *before* any Pillar 5 revert
decision is executed.** The revert decision and the correctness
evidence are independent products, and a reverted lever that was never
proven correct teaches the next arc nothing.

## Pillar 3 — The structural change lands alone, before any behavioural change

M2 introduces the shared runner-invocation helper and changes **no
command, no argument, and no environment variable**. M3–M6 then each
change exactly one target's invocation, as a small diff against a
single call site.

This ordering is the direct descendant of `34UpdatePlan.md`'s Pillar 3
(M3's accounting rewrite before M4's concurrency) and of
`33UpdatePlan.md`'s Pillar 8 before that. The reasoning transposes
cleanly: routing ten call sites through one helper is a wide-blast-
radius change with zero intended behavioural effect, which makes it
**perfectly checkable** — the harness's output must be byte-identical,
and any difference at all is a bug in the refactor. Landing a lever in
the same commit would destroy that property, because then a diff in the
output could mean either "the refactor lost something" or "the lever
changed something," and the debugging surface doubles at exactly the
moment it matters.

Landing them separately means M2's byte-identity proof is unambiguous,
and each lever inherits a call site already proven equivalent.

## Pillar 4 — Two baselines, so no delta is ever ambiguous

- **The frozen baseline**, recorded at M1 and never rewritten for the
  arc's duration: whole-run wall time at 2 reps, the five-target
  decomposition at 3 reps each, the marginal-cost fit per target, and
  the environment stamp. Every cumulative delta is measured against it.
- **The rolling per-milestone figures**, recorded in the running log,
  so each lever's own incremental contribution is visible rather than
  only the accumulated total.

`33UpdatePlan.md`'s M1 discovered that its inherited baseline disagreed
with reality by 37% on the binary that mattered most, and that four
independent readings of one quantity spanned 135s to 185s. That is the
direct precedent for this arc re-measuring the per-target decomposition
rather than inheriting `34UpdatePlan.md`'s — which, as this document's
opening section establishes, is known stale rather than merely
suspected stale.

## Pillar 5 — Rollback is graduated, pre-registered, and per-lever

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| Any non-java golden snapshot moves | **Revert.** Contract B breach, no graduated reading. |
| A java snapshot moves in a non-comment byte | **Revert.** The carve-out does not cover it. |
| More or fewer than 27 java snapshots move at M6 | **Stop and investigate** before proceeding; the count is pre-registered precisely so a surprise is caught. |
| `determinism.rs` fails | **Revert.** Contract B breach. |
| The harness's `[PASS]`/`[FAIL]` set changes | **Revert.** Contract A breach. |
| The harness reports green on a forced failure | **Revert immediately.** Contract D breach — carries no "disclose and keep" option. |
| `ciac sim --json` emits a malformed envelope | **Revert the offending lever.** FM3. |
| Any unrelated metric regresses beyond its noise band | **Revert**, unless the trade is stated and accepted in the milestone report. |
| A lever's gain < ½ its pre-registered threshold | **Revert that lever alone.** The complexity is not paid for. |
| A lever's gain ≥ ½ but < its threshold | **Keep that lever**, record the shortfall explicitly, and **measure the proposed root cause before recording it** (Pillar 1). |
| A lever's gain ≥ its threshold | **Keep.** |

**Levers revert independently.** This is new to this arc and follows
from having four of them. M3, M4, M5, and M6 are mutually independent
in content — each touches one target's two driver functions and nothing
else — so reverting one leaves the others intact and the shared helper
from M2 untouched. The running log records each lever's verdict
separately, and the checkpoint's cumulative figure is computed over
whatever survived.

**On ratio-shaped thresholds**, the half-point for a `≥T×` threshold is
`(T + 1) / 2`, inherited from `33UpdatePlan.md`'s Pillar 5. A ratio
threshold's zero point is 1.0×, not 0.

## Pillar 6 — The wrapper is not the work

**New to this arc, and its thesis.**

A build tool's job ends when the artifact is built. Invoking it again to
*run* that artifact re-does its startup work — dependency graph
resolution, fingerprint checking, plugin loading, environment
validation — none of which can produce a different answer than it
produced moments earlier, because nothing has changed in between.

Stated as a standing principle so a future target addition inherits it:

> When a `sim_drive_*` function has produced a runnable artifact, every
> subsequent scenario must be driven by executing that artifact
> directly. The build tool is not invoked again inside the scenario
> loop.

TypeScript has obeyed this since `23UpdatePlan.md` M9 gave it
`run_node_sim_runner` (`:2000`). Java's multi-service path has obeyed
it since `28UpdatePlan.md` M8 (`:2404-2407`). **This arc is therefore a
consistency argument, not a design proposal** — which is the strongest
form an argument in this repository can take, and the reason all four
levers carry an in-repo precedent column in the table above.

The principle has a natural boundary worth stating so it is not
over-applied: it governs the *scenario loop*, not the *build*. Nothing
here suggests bypassing `cargo build`, `npm ci`, `uv sync`, or `mvnw
test-compile`. Those invocations do work that must happen. It is only
their repetition inside a loop over scenarios that this pillar forbids.

## Pillar 7 — Reuse before invention

Inherited from `34UpdatePlan.md`, where lever 1's entire design was "use
the helper that already exists." It applies at least as directly here,
and in one case more so: **lever D's implementation already exists in
the same file, serving the same target** (`sim_drive_java_multi:2346-2407`).

The pillar's practical force is that reusing an existing mechanism
inherits decisions the implementer would otherwise have to re-make and
could get wrong. `java_multi` already decided how to invoke
`dependency:build-classpath` (`:2351`, via `-Dmdep.outputFile`), how to
assemble the classpath entries in the right order (`:2371-2378`,
`target/classes` then `target/test-classes` then resolved deps), and how
to separate them portably (`:2346`, `cfg!(windows)`). Lever D copies
those decisions rather than re-deriving them.

The same holds for lever C: `python_multi:1662-1672` already encodes
where a `uv`-managed venv lives and how to look inside it.

**Where invention is genuinely required, the document says so.** Lever
A's cargo-JSON artifact parsing has no in-repo precedent — nothing in
this repository currently reads `--message-format=json` output. That is
called out at M3 as the arc's one novel mechanism, and it is why M3
carries an explicit `--json`-envelope verification that the other
levers do not need.

## Pillar 8 — The checkpoint has real teeth, including the authority to stop

M7 is a hard serialization point: no work under M8 or M9 lands before
M7's own commit does.

Its four pre-registered outcomes:

- **(a) Proceed.** Cumulative gain clears the arc's headline, Contract
  D holds, no other contract is breached. M8 and M9 run as written.
- **(b) Proceed narrowed.** One or more levers underperformed and were
  reverted per Pillar 5; the rest ships. M8's documentation adjusts to
  describe what actually landed, naming the reverted levers with their
  numbers.
- **(c) Stop and ship what exists.** If the surviving levers deliver a
  gain too small to justify the permanent complexity of the shared
  helper plus four changed invocation paths, the arc may stop and ship
  only M2's refactor — which has independent value as de-duplication
  even at zero measured speedup. Recorded as a legitimate success,
  following `30UpdatePlan.md` M5 and `34UpdatePlan.md` M6, both of
  which stopped on these grounds and were right to.
- **(d) Stop and revert.** Every lever missed. The measured results go
  in the running log and the arc closes having established that
  per-scenario wrapper overhead is not where this harness's time goes —
  which is a real finding, and one nobody currently knows.

Note that outcome (c) has a floor this arc's predecessors lacked: even
if all four levers revert, **M2's refactor still ships**, because its
justification (ten copies of one protocol handler collapsing to one) is
independent of any performance claim.

## Pillar 9 — Instruments that must not move

`perf_baseline.rs`, `perf_scaling.rs`, and `perf_budget.rs` are
expected **flat** across this entire arc, and movement in any of them
means something unintended happened.

The reasoning is mechanical: those instruments measure compilation and
code generation. This arc changes how `ciac sim` *invokes external
processes after* generation completes. No code path that any of the
three measures is touched by any milestone. `perf_budget.rs` keeps
failing the build throughout and is neither weakened nor tightened.

This is stated as a pillar rather than a checklist line because a
flat-expected instrument that moves is more informative than a
threshold that misses — it means the change reached somewhere the
document did not predict, and that is a stop-and-investigate condition,
not a "record and proceed" one.

## The Contract B carve-out

This section exists because **this is the first arc in the series to
carve an exception into Contract B**, and an exception granted vaguely
is an exception that grows.

### Why one is needed

`34UpdatePlan.md`'s Contract B reads: "Every generated byte, on every
target, for every example, is identical before and after… Any snapshot
movement at all is a bug, with no disclosed exception anywhere in this
arc."

Lever D changes how java's single-service simulation is driven. Three
comment blocks in the java templates *describe that mechanism*, and
generated comments are generated bytes:

**1. `crates/ciac-backend-java/templates/pom.xml.j2:225-231`** — the
most affected generated text in the repository, because it names all
five targets' mechanisms explicitly:

> Running it via `test-compile exec:java` (build once, then one
> `exec:java` invocation per scenario) mirrors every other target's own
> "compile once, run the driver once per scenario" shape (Go's `go
> build` then `go run`; Rust's `cargo build` then a scoped `cargo run`;
> TypeScript's `npm run build` then `node dist/sim_runner.js`) without
> packaging a second artifact of its own.

**Four of those five descriptions become wrong after this arc.** Only
TypeScript's survives. A comment that describes the mechanism of every
target is a comment that four of five levers invalidate.

**2. `pom.xml.j2:37-40`** — "Not managed by spring-boot-dependencies'
own BOM, so pinned explicitly here (SimRunner's own `test-compile
exec:java` driver, 25UpdatePlan.md M9…)".

**3. `SimRunner.java.j2:14-17`** — "run via `mvn -q test-compile
exec:java` (see pom.xml's own `exec-maven-plugin` — compile once, then
one `exec:java` invocation per scenario, the same 'compile once, run the
driver once per scenario' shape every other target's own toolchain gives
for free)."

These appear in **27** java golden snapshots
(`tests/tests/snapshots/golden__gen__java__*.snap`), **27** of which
match the literal `exec:java`. In
`golden__gen__java__quickstart.snap` they are visible at lines 947,
1029-1030, and 3646-3647.

Leaving them stale would ship generated code describing a mechanism
`ciac sim` no longer uses — exactly what every prior arc's
documentation milestone exists to prevent. `34UpdatePlan.md` M7's own
exit criterion was "no documentation statement about this script's cost
or shape is left describing the pre-arc world." Generated comments are
documentation.

### The exception, stated precisely

1. **Comment-only.** Every non-comment byte in every snapshot must
   remain identical. The milestone's exit criterion is a reviewed diff
   demonstrating that every hunk falls inside an XML comment
   (`<!-- … -->`) or a Java line comment (`// …`).
2. **Java-only.** It applies to
   `tests/tests/snapshots/golden__gen__java__*.snap` and to nothing
   else. A python, rust, go, or typescript snapshot moving remains a
   Contract B breach with no graduated reading — Pillar 5's table gives
   that row no alternative.
3. **Counted in advance: 27, and the count was corrected while writing
   this document.** M6 re-states the count it expects to touch. A 26th
   or a 28th is a stop-and-investigate condition, not a number to be
   absorbed.

   The correction is worth recording, because it is the
   pre-registration mechanism working before any code existed. An
   earlier draft of this section stated **29** — the total number of
   `golden__gen__java__*.snap` files — on the assumption that every
   java snapshot carries the affected comments. It does not.
   `golden__gen__java__modular-video.snap` and
   `golden__gen__java__ping.snap` contain **no `SimRunner` and no
   `exec-maven-plugin` at all**: those two examples declare nothing the
   simulation world can fake (no `db`/`queue` use), so
   `SimRunner.java.j2` is never rendered for them, the
   `exec-maven-plugin` block is gated out of their POM, and with it the
   `pom.xml.j2:37-40` and `:225-231` comment blocks. All three affected
   comments are absent, so **those two files cannot move**.

   Verified directly: 29 java snapshots exist, 27 contain `exec:java`,
   and the 2 that do not are exactly those two files. The carve-out is
   therefore bounded to **27**, and a diff touching 29 would mean
   something reached the two examples that have no simulation runner —
   which would be a real defect, not a rounding difference.
4. **Spent by exactly one milestone.** M6 and no other. M2, M3, M4, M5
   touch no template and must show zero snapshot movement of any kind.
5. **The `exec-maven-plugin` block itself stays.**
   `pom.xml.j2:232-240` remains in the generated POM. It is still valid
   for manual invocation, and removing it would convert a comment
   change into a functional generated-output change — which the

## Determinism and supply chain

**No new dependencies.** Not one crate is added to `Cargo.toml`; no new
entry appears in `Cargo.lock` except the version bump's own
regeneration at M9. No new external tool is required — every lever uses
a binary the corresponding toolchain already installed. `deny.toml` is
untouched.

**Cargo's JSON message format is a stable interface, not a scrape.**
`--message-format=json` is a documented, versioned cargo output mode
with a `reason` field discriminating message kinds; lever A reads
`compiler-artifact` messages' `executable` field. The
`json-render-diagnostics` variant specifically keeps human-readable
diagnostics on stderr while emitting structured messages on stdout,
which is what preserves the current build-progress UX. This is
recorded because "parse the tool's output" is otherwise a smell, and
the distinction between a stable structured interface and screen-
scraping is the whole difference.

**Compiler determinism is not at risk.** No lever changes what is
generated (except three comments), what is compiled, or with what
flags. Lever A changes cargo's *output format*, not its build
configuration — the same artifacts are produced with the same
fingerprints. Lever B changes where go writes a binary it was already
producing. Levers C and D change which process is asked to start an
interpreter or JVM that was going to start anyway.

## The failure-mode register

`34UpdatePlan.md` carried eleven entries because its lever 2 introduced
concurrency, and eight of those eleven existed only because of it —
lost counter increments across process boundaries, orphaned
grandchildren, output interleaving, cargo lock contention, and so on.
**This arc introduces no concurrency, so those eight do not apply**, and
the register is correspondingly smaller. Saying that explicitly is
better than padding the register to look thorough.

Six entries remain, each with its mechanism, resolution, completeness
label, and named verification.

### FM1 — Stale binary reuse (the dangerous one)

**Mechanism.** Every lever replaces "ask the build tool to run it" with
"run this file." If the file is stale — left over from a previous build
that the current build did not actually refresh — the simulator runs
the *wrong code* and reports `[PASS]` or `[FAIL]` about a program that
is not the one under test. Unlike a build failure, this is silent: the
run completes, emits well-formed JSON, and is indistinguishable from a
correct result.

This is the direct analogue of `34UpdatePlan.md`'s FM1, and it earns
the same "dangerous one" label for the same reason: it produces a
confident wrong answer rather than an error.

**Resolution — mitigated under the harness, honestly uncovered
outside it.** Three layers, of decreasing strength:

1. **Cargo and `go build` are content-addressed and correct.** Both
   fingerprint their inputs and rebuild when those change. A build that
   no-ops did so because nothing changed, in which case the existing
   binary *is* current. Lever A additionally reads the artifact path
   out of the build's own output, so it cannot name a file the build
   did not just vouch for.
2. **The harness deletes every project between combinations**
   (`sim-corpus-x5.sh:111`, `:116`), so under `sim-corpus-x5.sh` every
   build is cold with respect to the generated project and no stale
   artifact can exist.
3. **The uncovered case is a developer re-running `ciac sim` against a
   persistent `--out` directory** after editing the `.ciac` source. Here
   the mitigation rests entirely on layer 1 — which is sound for cargo
   and go, sound for `javac` output under Maven's incremental compile,
   and weakest for the python path, where no compilation occurs at all
   and the interpreter simply reads whatever `.py` files are present.

**Completeness: mitigated, not eliminated.** The register keeps one
entry honestly labelled below "entirely," and this is it.
`34UpdatePlan.md`'s retrospective (`:1926-1942`) records that its own
register was never adversarially exercised because the milestone that
would have exercised it was reverted first; this document inherits that
warning and responds to it by naming the uncovered case precisely
rather than claiming coverage it has not tested.

**Verification.** M2–M6 each run the harness, where layer 2 holds
absolutely. Additionally, at M7: generate a project to a persistent
`--out`, run `ciac sim`, edit the source program materially, re-run
against the same `--out`, and confirm the second run reflects the edit.
Recorded with its result rather than asserted.

### FM2 — Binary path derivation drift

**Mechanism.** Three of the four levers must name a file the build
produced. If the naming convention is wrong — or right today and wrong
after a toolchain update — the driver fails to find an artifact that
exists, or finds the wrong one.

**Resolution — dissolved for rust, convention-based elsewhere with
in-repo precedent.** Lever A does not derive a path at all; it reads
the `executable` field cargo itself emits, so there is no convention to
drift. Lever B names the path it passes to `go build -o`, so the
driver chose it and cannot be wrong about it. Lever C's
`.venv/bin/python` is already assumed by `python_multi:1662-1672`,
which globs inside that same directory today — the lever adds no new
assumption. Lever D's `target/test-classes` glob is the only genuinely
new convention, and it is checked at runtime with a clear bail rather
than assumed.

**Completeness: dissolved (rust, go), by existing precedent (python),
newly introduced with an explicit guard (java).**

**Verification.** Each lever's own milestone runs the full 50-combination
harness, which exercises every path on every program.

### FM3 — `JSON_MODE` output contamination

**Mechanism.** `run_streamed` (`:192`) checks a global `JSON_MODE` flag
and, when set, captures the child's output and writes **both streams to
stderr**. `ciac sim --json` sets that flag. Lever A changes the rust
build to emit machine-readable JSON on stdout. If that build still goes
through `run_streamed` under `JSON_MODE`, the artifact JSON is written
to stderr — where it is at best noise and at worst confuses a consumer
parsing the envelope. If instead the lever captures stdout itself but
forgets the `JSON_MODE` interaction, build diagnostics may vanish.

**Resolution — entirely, by not routing the JSON build through
`run_streamed` at all.** Lever A introduces its own capture for the
build step, parses artifact messages from stdout, and forwards stderr
using the same mechanism the existing drivers use for child stderr
(`:1580-1583` and twins). `--message-format=json-render-diagnostics`
specifically keeps human-readable diagnostics on stderr, so nothing a
developer currently sees is lost.

**Completeness: entirely**, provided the verification below runs.

**Verification.** M3's non-negotiable extra exit criterion: `ciac sim
--json` on at least one rust program, envelope parsed and confirmed
well-formed, with no `compiler-artifact` text anywhere in it. Run
against both `_single` and `_multi` paths.

### FM4 — FQCN glob ambiguity

**Mechanism.** Lever D resolves java's main class by globbing
`target/test-classes` for `SimRunner.class`. If a generated project
ever contains more than one — a nested class file, a second package, a
test fixture named identically — the glob picks arbitrarily and the
driver runs the wrong entry point.

**Resolution — entirely, by failing loudly on ambiguity.** The glob
collects **all** matches and bails with a clear message naming every
candidate if the count is not exactly one, rather than taking the
first. A zero-match case bails with the same clarity, pointing at
`docs/simulation.md` the way the existing guards do (`:2255-2259`).

**Completeness: entirely.** An ambiguous state cannot produce a silent
wrong answer; it produces an error naming the ambiguity.

**Verification.** M6 runs the full java stream across all ten programs,
seven of which take the `_single` path. The guard's zero-match and
multi-match branches are additionally exercised by temporary injection
at M6 and reverted, recorded in the running log.

### FM5 — Venv layout assumption

**Mechanism.** Lever C assumes `uv sync` produces
`<project>/.venv/bin/python`. If `uv` is configured to place the venv
elsewhere (`UV_PROJECT_ENVIRONMENT`), or the platform uses
`Scripts/python.exe`, the path is wrong.

**Resolution — entirely, by checking rather than assuming, with a
documented fallback.** The lever probes for the interpreter at the
conventional location and, if absent, **falls back to the existing `uv
run` invocation** rather than failing. That fallback is the current
behaviour, so a non-standard environment gets today's performance and
today's correctness rather than an error. Windows uses
`Scripts/python.exe` under `cfg!(windows)`, following `:2346`'s idiom.

**Completeness: entirely**, and notably this is the only lever with a
graceful degradation path — because it is the only one where the
pre-lever mechanism remains available at zero cost.

**Verification.** M5 runs the full python stream. The fallback branch is
exercised by temporarily pointing `UV_PROJECT_ENVIRONMENT` elsewhere
and confirming the driver still succeeds, recorded in the running log.

### FM6 — Contract B over-reach

**Mechanism.** The carve-out permits comment-only movement in java
snapshots. A careless implementation could let a *functional* change
ride along inside those 27 files — a reordered dependency, a changed
plugin version, a whitespace-normalised block — and the reviewer, told
that snapshot movement is expected here, absorbs it.

This is a failure mode of the *process*, not the code, and it is the
one this arc introduces that no predecessor had.

**Resolution — entirely, by construction of the check.** M6's exit
criterion is not "snapshots updated" but "every hunk in the diff falls
inside a comment delimiter, verified hunk by hunk, across exactly 27
files." The count is pre-registered (27), so an unexpected file is
caught by arithmetic before anyone reads a diff. Non-java snapshots
remain under the absolute rule, so the blast radius of a mistake is
bounded to one target.

**Completeness: entirely**, conditional on the review actually being
performed hunk by hunk — which is why it is written as the milestone's
exit criterion rather than as advice.

**Verification.** M6's diff review, recorded in the running log with
the actual file count and a statement that every hunk was inside a
comment. M7 re-confirms via a full workspace test run.

## Open questions resolved at implementation (pre-registered)

Recorded now, with their decision rules, so the answers cannot be
reverse-engineered from whatever turns out to be convenient.

1. **How does lever A learn the rust binary's path — parse cargo's JSON
   output, or derive it by convention?** Resolved in advance: **parse
   the JSON.** The build already runs, so the structured output is
   free; it eliminates path derivation as a failure mode entirely
   (FM2); and it is the only mechanism that works for
   `sim_drive_rust_multi`, which builds without a `--bin` selector
   (`:1859`) and therefore produces a binary whose name comes from
   `system-runner`'s own `Cargo.toml` — a value the driver never
   otherwise learns. **Reversal rule:** if `JSON_MODE` contamination
   (FM3) proves unresolvable without restructuring `run_streamed`, fall
   back to convention-based derivation for `_single` and read
   `system-runner/Cargo.toml`'s `[package] name` for `_multi`, and
   record the reversal as a deviation.

2. **Should the shared helper from M2 own the *build* step too, or only
   the scenario loop?** Resolved: **only the scenario loop.** The five
   targets' build steps have nothing in common — one runs cargo, one
   npm twice, one uv, one go, one Maven — and forcing them through a
   shared abstraction would produce a parameter bag rather than a
   helper. The scenario loop, by contrast, is genuinely identical
   across all ten drivers. Pillar 7 favours reusing what fits, not
   maximising what is shared.

3. **Does lever C fall back or fail when `.venv/bin/python` is
   absent?** Resolved: **fall back to `uv run`.** Unique among the four
   levers, python's pre-lever mechanism remains available at no cost,
   so a non-standard environment gets today's behaviour instead of an
   error. **Rule:** the fallback is silent in normal operation but is
   exercised and recorded at M5, so its existence is proven rather than
   assumed.

4. **Should `sim-corpus-selftest.sh` be built, per
   `34UpdatePlan.md`'s open question #2?** Resolved: **no, and it is
   re-handed-off rather than dropped.** That question named it "the top
   candidate for the *next* arc's inventory," and this is the next arc.
   But the property it checks is the *harness's tally* — the thing
   `34UpdatePlan.md` M3 rewrote — and this arc does not touch the
   harness's tally at all. Building a permanent artifact to guard code
   this arc leaves alone is scope creep. Contract D continues to run as
   a procedure at every boundary. The item is recorded again in the
   handoff, with the observation that it has now been deferred twice
   and that the arc which finally changes the tally again should take
   it.

5. **What happens if a sixth target is added to the corpus?** The
   design scales without change: the new driver uses M2's shared helper
   for its scenario loop, and Pillar 6 tells its author not to invoke
   the build tool inside that loop. Recorded so a future target
   addition inherits the principle rather than re-deriving it — or
   worse, copying whichever existing driver it happens to look at
   first, which is exactly how java ended up with two different answers
   in one file.

6. **If two levers both miss but their combination would clear a
   cumulative bar, do they stay?** Resolved in advance: **no.** Pillar
   5's table is evaluated per-lever, and a lever below its own
   half-point reverts regardless of what the aggregate looks like.
   Permitting aggregate rescue would reintroduce exactly the fitted-
   threshold problem Pillar 1 forbids, one level up.

## Explicit cuts

- **No concurrency, in any form.** `34UpdatePlan.md`'s reverted lever 2
  and its two named successors — bounded streams below the core count,
  and rust-first two-phase scheduling — stay handed off. Cut here not
  because they are wrong but because mixing mechanism classes would
  make a disappointing cumulative number un-attributable, and because
  this arc changes the serial denominator any future concurrency arc
  would be measured against. **Recorded with the observation that 35
  makes 36's eventual number more honest, not less.**
- **No single-invocation multi-scenario runners.** Passing all N
  scenario paths to one runner process would collapse 75 process starts
  to 50 across the corpus — strictly more than every lever in this arc
  combined, since it removes process startup rather than merely wrapper
  overhead. Cut because it requires changing every runner template's
  argument handling, which is a real generated-code change far beyond
  the comment-only carve-out, with 145 snapshots moving instead of 27.
  **Recorded as the highest-value follow-up in this mechanism class.**
- **No `npm ci` flag tuning.** `commands.rs:1951` runs bare `npm ci`
  with no `--prefer-offline --no-audit --no-fund`. Audit and fund are
  network round-trips paid ten times per run. Named and sized here so
  the next arc need not rediscover it; not taken because TypeScript is
  the one target this arc holds up as already correct, and changing its
  build step would muddle that argument.
- **No java classpath caching.** Computing `dependency:build-classpath`
  once and reusing it would remove lever D's +7 penalty entirely, but
  classpaths **cannot** be shared blindly — `pom.xml.j2:37-45` gates
  dependencies on `has_logging` and similar flags, so two generated
  projects may resolve differently. Doing it correctly requires keying
  a cache on POM content hash, which is real machinery for a lever that
  may not even clear its gate. Priced separately, not bundled.
- **No AppCDS for the SimRunner JVM.** `33UpdatePlan.md` M6 proved the
  technique on the java formatter's JVM. It composes with lever D — but
  only *after* lever D puts java on bare `java` invocations, since
  there is no point archiving class metadata for a JVM that Maven is
  starting. Recorded as lever D's natural successor.
- **No `CARGO_PROFILE_DEV_DEBUG=0`.** Debug info generation and linking
  is a large share of a debug build, so this is tempting. Cut because
  it would fingerprint-thrash against `ciac verify`, which shares
  `/tmp/ciac-verify-cargo-target` (`:3741`) and does not set it — every
  alternation between `verify` and `sim` would force a full rebuild.
  Isolating it needs a second multi-GB target tree, and the disk has
  **9.7 GB free at 74% utilisation** with the existing tree already at
  6.2 GB. Measured, not assumed.
- **No tmpfs `WORKDIR`.** `/tmp` is ext4 on `/dev/vda` on this machine,
  not a RAM filesystem, and there is neither the free disk nor the
  spare RAM to make it one alongside a 6.2 GB target tree in 16 GB
  total. **On this machine** — the idea is not wrong in general, and
  the document says so rather than dismissing it.
- **No changes to the corpus.** Which programs, which scenarios, and
  which pairings are all untouched. This arc makes the same 75
  scenario-runs faster; it does not make them fewer.
- **No CI wiring.** The harness stays run-on-demand.

## Risks

- **The java lever may not be worth its Contract B cost.** This is the
  arc's most structurally interesting change — propagating a correct
  implementation over an incorrect one in the same file — and also its
  least certain win, since it trades N Maven bootstraps for N JVM
  starts at the cost of one extra Maven call per program, and six of
  seven single-service java programs carry only one scenario. M1's gate
  exists precisely because "structurally satisfying" and "measurably
  faster" are different properties and this document declines to
  conflate them.
- **This is the first Contract B exception in the series.** Four arcs
  have held it absolute. Carving one — even narrowly — establishes that
  it *can* be carved, and the next arc will find it easier to ask. The
  mitigations are that this one is comment-only, java-only, counted in
  advance, spent by one milestone, and reviewed hunk by hunk; the
  residual risk is cultural rather than technical and is named here
  rather than minimised.
- **M2 touches all ten driver functions at once.** The blast radius is
  the widest in the arc even though the intended behavioural change is
  nil — which is exactly why it lands alone with a byte-identity exit
  criterion, and why bundling it with a lever was rejected.
- **Lever A introduces the repository's first structured-output
  parsing of a build tool.** Nothing here currently reads
  `--message-format=json`. The interface is stable and documented, but
  there is no in-repo precedent to copy and no second example a
  reviewer can compare against — the same "first of its kind" risk
  `34UpdatePlan.md` flagged for bash parallelism, and it is mitigated
  the same way: an explicit extra exit criterion (the `--json` envelope
  check) that no other lever carries.
- **Four levers, four thresholds, one shared codebase.** A lever that
  reverts after a later one has landed is awkward to unwind. Mitigated
  by their content-independence — each touches one target's two
  functions — and by pre-registering that they revert individually.
- **Every ratio here will be measured on 4 vCPUs**, 16 GB, per
  `docs/perf/baseline.json`. Unlike `34UpdatePlan.md`'s lever 2, none
  of these levers is CPU-bound or contention-sensitive, so the core
  count is not expected to bind — but that expectation is itself
  unmeasured, and this document labels it a hypothesis rather than a
  finding, per Pillar 1.
- **The thresholds do not exist yet.** They are derived at M1 from a
  rule fixed in this document. That is a stricter application of Pillar
  1 than any predecessor attempted, and it carries its own risk: if
  M1's `m_t` fit is noisy, four thresholds inherit that noise at once.
  Mitigated by fitting across four programs and three distinct scenario
  counts rather than differencing two readings, and by 3 reps at each
  point.

## M1 note — what the measurements actually said, and the narrowing they force

**M1 ran in full and produced three findings, two of which were not
anticipated by this document and one of which invalidates its sizing.**

### Finding 1 — "the baseline" is not one number; it is a cache state

Two full-harness reps taken back to back disagreed by **43%**: rep 1
**952s**, rep 2 **544s**. Contract A's sorted diff between them was
byte-identical, so this is timing spread, not a correctness problem —
and it is far outside the ~3% agreement `34UpdatePlan.md`'s own reps
achieved.

The cause was measured, not hypothesised (Pillar 1). During rep 1 the
shared cargo target directory grew **6.2 GB → 12 GB** and
`~/.cache/go-build` reached **11 GB**; rep 2 reused all of it. The
mechanism matters beyond this arc: the shared target dir caches **each
generated project's own compiled crate**, not merely shared
dependencies, because every program's `sim_runner` resolves to a
distinct metadata hash via its distinct manifest path. All ten persist
across runs.

Two consequences:

- **`34UpdatePlan.md`'s lever 1 is worth considerably more than its
  reported 3.07×** on repeat corpus runs. This document's own opening
  section claimed rust was ~40% of the run post-lever-1; **warm, rust
  is 7.4%**. That claim is hereby corrected by measurement, and the
  correction runs in the same direction as — but much further than —
  the correction this document made to its predecessor.
- **That arc's headline figures were semi-cold readings.** Rep 1's 952s
  matches its M8 close-out (949.3s) to within 0.3%, while a genuinely
  warm run is ~550s. Neither number is wrong; they measure different
  situations, and no document in this series had previously noticed
  that the situation is a variable.

A warm steady state was adopted as the frozen baseline — it is
reproducible, it is the harder test for these levers, and a cold run's
magnitude depends on unknowable history.

**Disk, separately: `34UpdatePlan.md`'s FM5 fired for real.** Two full
runs took utilisation from 74% to **91% (3.8 GB free)**, breaching this
document's own pre-flight rule mid-milestone. Reclaimed via `go clean
-cache`; notably the go cache returned at only **801 MB** after a
subsequent full run, so the 11 GB was accumulated cruft from the whole
34 arc rather than one run's working set. Recorded because the rule was
breached by the measurement procedure itself, which no prior arc
anticipated.

### Finding 2 — the first marginal-cost fit was contaminated, and the residual check caught it

The pre-registered design fitted `T = b + m·n` across four
single-service programs carrying 1, 2, 2, and 4 scenarios. Run as
specified, it produced **`m_java` = 26.27 s/scenario and a negative
intercept of −31.67s** — a physically impossible build cost, which is
what exposed the problem.

The four raw java points were 1 → 7.59s, 2 → 11.03s, 2 → 11.22s,
4 → **79.90s**. That is not a line. `sim-peripherals` is a
high-leverage outlier whose *build* is expensive (object store, email,
search, cache, HTTP fixtures, auth scopes — roughly 67s of javac and
Maven work), and a cross-program fit was measuring build-cost variation
rather than per-scenario cost. This document's instruction to "record
the residuals, not just the slope" is what surfaced it; the automated
confidence heuristic did **not**, because it normalised residuals
against an `m` the outlier had already inflated. That heuristic is
noted here as inadequate rather than quietly fixed.

**Corrected design, and the one now of record.** Hold the program *and
the scenario content* fixed and vary only how many times the runner is
invoked, by passing the same scenario file N times (N = 1, 2, 4, 8).
Build cost, codegen, and scenario semantics are then identical across
all four points, so the slope is exactly the per-invocation wrapper
overhead these levers remove. Verified that the CLI accepts repeated
`--scenario` arguments and emits one result per invocation.

| Target | n=1 | n=2 | n=4 | n=8 | **m (s/invocation)** | intercept | max&#124;resid&#124;/m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| java | 7.78 | 11.00 | 17.18 | 29.94 | **3.162** | 4.62 | 0.03 |
| typescript | 10.09 | 12.05 | 13.45 | 15.49 | **0.708** | 10.11 | 1.03 |
| python | 1.90 | 2.61 | 3.92 | 6.79 | **0.697** | 1.19 | 0.09 |
| rust | 3.39 | 3.33 | 3.76 | 4.35 | **0.149** | 3.15 | 0.79 |
| go | 1.16 | 1.24 | 1.54 | 2.05 | **0.130** | 1.01 | 0.23 |

`m_java` falls from 26.27 to **3.162** — a Maven bootstrap costs about
3.2 seconds, which is credible where 26 was not. The contaminated
figure was **7.8× too high**, and had it gone unchallenged it would
have produced a java threshold of 6.07× that no implementation could
ever have met, followed by a "root cause" investigation of a shortfall
that was an artefact of the measurement.

### The narrowing, taken at M1 by Pillar 5's own logic

Pillar 5's table reverts a lever whose gain falls below half its
threshold. Applying that rule *before* implementation is strictly
better than after, and this document's M1 was explicitly given the
authority to decide a lever's fate on measurement — it simply expected
to use it on java rather than on rust and go.

- **Lever A (rust) — cut.** 1.3s. It is also the arc's single most
  complex change: the only novel mechanism (cargo JSON artifact
  parsing), the only one carrying a named contamination risk (FM3,
  `JSON_MODE`), and the only one requiring an extra non-negotiable exit
  criterion. Spending that on 1.3s is indefensible.
- **Lever B (go) — cut.** 1.2s, against the arc's only API break
  (`run_go_sim_runner`'s signature). Note the wasted `-o /dev/null`
  build is real and remains genuinely wasteful; it is simply worth
  ~1s across the whole corpus because go's total stream is 12.3s.
- **Lever C (python) — retained.** 6.3s absolute is small, but it is
  **24.2% of python's own stream** — the best ratio in the arc — and it

## Retrospective

Written at M9, against M1's frozen baseline. The questions this arc
commits to answering, chosen so that a disappointing result is as
reportable as a good one:

1. **What was the cumulative wall-time delta, and what did each lever
   contribute separately?** Stated per lever, with reverted levers
   listed at their measured value rather than omitted.
2. **Did M1's derived thresholds turn out to be well-calibrated?** A
   rule fixed before its inputs were known is a real experiment; if
   every lever cleared its threshold comfortably or every lever missed,
   the 60%-of-ideal factor was wrong and the next arc should know.
3. **Was the per-scenario marginal-cost model accurate?** Compare each
   lever's measured saving against `0.6 × m_t × N_t`. A systematic gap
   in either direction is a finding about the model, not about the
   levers.
4. **Did the Contract B carve-out hold its bounds?** Exactly 27 files,
   every hunk in a comment, zero non-java movement — or not, and if
   not, what leaked and why.
5. **Did Contract D catch anything the timing numbers did not?**
   `34UpdatePlan.md`'s answer was "twice, and both were real findings."
   This arc's answer may well be "no," and that is a legitimate
   result worth recording rather than a gap to apologise for.
6. **Did the failure-mode register's completeness labels survive the
   verifications this document attached to them?** Specifically FM1,
   the only entry labelled *mitigated* — did the persistent-`--out`
   check at M7 confirm the uncovered case is as narrow as claimed?
7. **Was the decision to exclude concurrency correct?** With a faster
   serial baseline now measured, is the case for a bounded-concurrency
   arc stronger or weaker than `34UpdatePlan.md` left it?
8. **What does this arc hand off**, and to whom — including anything it
   learned that invalidates a claim in this document.
